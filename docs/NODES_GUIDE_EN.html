⚠ Safety — charging gets hot

Orbs become quite hot while charging. This is normal for wireless charging, but it means charging must be attended.

Never leave the charger supply running unattended — not overnight, and not while the room is empty. Switch the charger supply off when you leave.

Keep the lid open while charging so heat can escape, and do not cover the case with fabric or paper.

A warm orb is fine. An orb too hot to hold comfortably is not — take it off the charger and let it cool.

Two ways to connect. Prefer the cable.

RouteAddressWhen to use it
USB-C cable10.55.0.1Preferred. Faster, more reliable, and it does not use up one of the station's scarce Wi-Fi seats.
Wi-Fi hotspot10.0.0.8Only when a cable is impractical. The radio holds about eight devices in total and the orbs need those seats — every laptop that joins may cost you an orb.

The real box

Power up

The two power supplies are independent. The USB-C one runs the computer; the other runs the six charging wells.

1

Plug the USB-C power supply into the port on the side of the case, then into the wall.

The screen in the lid lights up on its own. Nothing to press.

Wait about 60 seconds for the station to start
2

Plug the charger power supply into the round socket, then into the wall.

⚠ Only do this while someone is present — see the safety note on page 2. Switch it off when you leave the room.

3

Lift an orb out and shake it to wake it.

It joins within a couple of seconds. Repeat for as many orbs as you want live.

An orb that is not charging goes to sleep after 30 seconds of stillness and disappears from the data until shaken. This is normal.

✓

Leave orbs in the wells between sessions, with the charger on and someone present. While actually charging they stay awake.

✗

Do not power the station from a laptop USB port if you can avoid it. A weak port browns the computer out and the link dies. Use the supplied wall supply.

Sound

Getting sound out

The orbs make their own sound, and the station also produces a full mix of the whole group. That mix comes out of a 3.5 mm jack — but not the one you expect.

Plug speakers or headphones into the 3.5 mm socket on the screen, in the lid.

⚠ Not the socket on the small computer. That one is switched off in software and will always be silent, no matter what you plug into it. The sound travels to the screen through the internal video cable.

Powered speakers work best. Set the volume on the speakers themselves — the station has no volume control of its own.

Want to drive your own instruments instead? The same USB-C cable also carries MIDI.

Your laptop sees a MIDI device called orbstation with no setup. Select it as a MIDI input in TouchDesigner, Ableton or anything else.

What arrives on MIDI

MIDI channelthe group (cluster) — 1st group → channel 1, 2nd → channel 2, and so on. Route each to a different instrument.
Low notes (around C3)a swelling pad — an orb being spun
High notes (around C5)a chime — an orb being jolted or thrown

So you can take the station's own mix from the screen's jack, or ignore it entirely and build your own sound from the MIDI. Both at once is fine.

Important

Charging orbs vanish from the screen

This surprises everyone, so it is worth knowing before it confuses you.

The station's own display — the screen in the lid, and the proximity graph on it — deliberately hides any orb that is sitting on a charger. So a case full of charging orbs shows an empty screen. Nothing is broken.

But they are still in the data. Charging orbs come through to TouchDesigner as normal, flagged so you can choose what to do with them.

WhereA charging orb…
Screen in the lidis hidden entirely
Your TouchDesigner patcharrives as normal, with charging = 1
The / status pageis listed, marked charging

So: to see orbs on the screen in the lid, take them out of the wells. To decide in your patch, filter on charging.

You can test this without the box: ./run.sh 6 --charging 2 marks two simulated orbs as docked.

TouchDesigner

Route 1 — OSC, no code

The quickest way in. Two steps, no scripting, every orb as ordinary CHOP channels. Start here.

7

Visit this address once, in any browser:

http://10.55.0.1:8080/osc/subscribe?port=7000

This tells the station to stream to your machine. It is permanent — you can close the browser. Use 127.0.0.1 instead for the simulator.

8

In TouchDesigner, add an OSC In CHOP and set Network Port to 7000.

Channels appear straight away. Nothing else to configure.

What you get

ChannelMeaning
orb/a1b2c3/accel1..3Accelerometer x, y, z — in g, gravity included
orb/a1b2c3/gyro1..3Gyroscope x, y, z — degrees per second

Route 2 — WebSocket, full data

Use this when you want the complete picture — in particular the whole proximity grid as a fixed matrix. Three ready-made scripts are supplied in touchdesigner/.

9

Add a WebSocket DAT.

Network Address10.55.0.1
Network Port8080
Request URL/ws

Paste websocket_callbacks.py into its callbacks. Then add two Script CHOPs beside it named orbs and proximity, and paste the two matching scripts.

Understanding proximity

Each orb listens for every other orb on a short-range radio and reports how strongly it hears them. That is the proximity data. It is measured by the orbs themselves — not worked out from positions, and nothing to do with the Wi-Fi signal.

0 = cannot hear it  ·  255 (or 1.0 normalised) = touching. It is not a distance: it falls off unevenly, it is noisy, and bodies between two orbs weaken it. Treat it as togetherness, not metres.

Expect saturation. On the firmware currently in the fleet, orbs closer than about half a metre all report near 255. A tight huddle therefore looks uniformly "touching" with little structure inside it. Spread the orbs out to see the gradient.

✓

Key everything on the orb's serial (a1b2c3). It is printed on the orb and never changes.

✗

Do not rely on slot numbers or a fixed orb count. Orbs join and drop out mid-session, and slots are reassigned on restart.

Important

Simulated versus real

The program serving the simulated data is the same one the real station runs — only the orbs are imaginary. Every field name and address is identical. But imaginary orbs behave too well.

✓ What the simulator gets right

Clusters genuinely form, split and merge. Proximity saturates close-up exactly as the real firmware does. Orbs cycle through rest, shake and spin, and occasionally jump. Battery, signal, firmware version and packet timing all match the real ranges.

What it gets wrong — design for these

Not simulatedWhy it matters on the day
Orbs dropping outA real orb that is not charging sleeps after 30 seconds of stillness and vanishes until shaken. Your patch must survive orbs coming and going. This is the one that catches people out.
Packet lossSignal is perfect here. A room full of bodies on a congested 2.4 GHz band is not. Smooth your inputs.
Battery drainingVoltages are static — nothing drains or fills. Do not build a piece that depends on battery level without testing on hardware.
HeatReal orbs warm up in the wells and the case needs air. Plan for attended charging breaks in a long installation.
✓

Test with ./run.sh 12 and again with ./run.sh 2. If it survives both, it will survive the room.

✗

Do not assume a fixed orb count, and do not key anything on slot numbers.

After a session

Taking the data away

The station records every session. Those recordings live in memory and are erased when it powers off — copy them before you unplug anything.

10

In a terminal on your laptop:

scp nodes@10.55.0.1:orb_logs/* .

Password is pass. Use 10.0.0.8 if you are on Wi-Fi. This copies every recording into the current folder.

FileOne row per…Columns
run-001.csvorb, per sampletime, serial, cluster, accelerometer, gyroscope, hue, charging, battery, signal, nearest orb
run-001.prox.csvpair of orbs, per sampletime, orb A, orb B, strength 0–255 — the full proximity graph over the whole session

Both are plain CSV, ten samples a second. Join them on the t column. Time is seconds since the recording started — the station has no clock.

Shutting down

Powering off

11

Once you have copied your recordings — just pull the power out.

No shutdown sequence is needed; the station's disk is protected against sudden power loss by design. Switch the charger supply off too — never leave it running unattended.

✗

Do not unplug before copying. Recordings are held in memory and are gone the instant power is lost.

✓

Copy first, unplug second. The files are small — copying twice costs nothing.

If something is wrong

Troubleshooting

Work down the list. Almost everything is one of the first three rows.

What you seeWhat to do
The screen in the lid shows no orbsAlmost always because they are charging — the display hides those on purpose (page 7). Lift one out and shake it. They are still in your data either way.
The browser cannot reach :8080 at allWrong address for your route. Cable → 10.55.0.1, Wi-Fi → 10.0.0.8, simulator → 127.0.0.1. If still nothing, try a different USB port — a weak port is the single most common cause.
Page loads, but says no orbsThe orbs are asleep — one that is not charging drops off after 30 seconds of stillness. Lift one out and shake it. If a good shake does nothing, that orb is flat: charge it for an hour, with someone present.
OSC channels never appearVisit the /osc/subscribe?port=7000 address again, then open /health — your machine's address should be listed under osc_targets. Check the port matches.
Data arrives, then freezesOpen /health. If ok is false, unplug the station, wait ten seconds, plug it back in.
A prox/… channel vanishedNormal. Those two orbs stopped hearing each other. It returns when they are close again. Use Route 2 for a fixed-size grid.
Orbs keep dropping off the Wi-FiToo many devices. The radio holds about eight in total. Take laptops and phones off OrbAP and use the cable.
An orb is very hotTake it off the charger and let it cool. Warm is normal while charging; too hot to hold is not. Never charge unattended.
No sound from the speakersCheck you are plugged into the jack on the screen, not the one on the small computer — that one is switched off and is always silent (page 6). Then check the speakers' own volume.
Everything is strange and you want to start againUnplug both supplies, wait ten seconds, plug them back in. The station rebuilds itself on every start — you cannot break it this way.
